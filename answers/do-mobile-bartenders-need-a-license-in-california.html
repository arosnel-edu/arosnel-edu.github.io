Answers

Do mobile bartenders need a license in California?

Every person who serves alcohol in California must hold Responsible Beverage Service certification and be registered with the Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control. A separate liquor licence is only required if the bartender is selling the alcohol, which is why most mobile bars operate on a dry hire basis where the host buys it.

Two different things people confuse

There are two separate requirements, and conflating them causes most of the confusion in this category. The first is server certification, which applies to the individual pouring the drink. The second is a liquor licence, which applies to the business selling the drink. A mobile bartender needs the first. They only need the second if money is changing hands for alcohol.

RBS certification: required for the person

Under California law, alcohol servers and their managers must complete Responsible Beverage Service training from a provider accredited by ABC, pass an exam, and register in the ABC RBS portal. The requirement took effect in July 2022 and applies broadly to anyone serving alcoholic beverages for on-premises consumption at a licensed premises or event.

The training itself covers checking identification, recognising and refusing service to intoxicated guests, understanding liability, and handling the moments where saying no is uncomfortable. It is not onerous, and the fact that a service has bothered to certify every bartender tells you something about how they operate. Certification is time-limited and has to be renewed, so it is fair to ask when your bartender last recertified.

Every FYRE Bartending bartender is RBS-certified.

Liquor licences: required for the sale

Selling or reselling alcohol requires an ABC licence. A mobile bartending company that does not hold one cannot include alcohol in your package, cannot mark it up, and cannot run a cash bar where guests pay for drinks. This is exactly why the dry hire model dominates private events: you purchase the alcohol as a private individual, your guests are not charged, and no sale occurs.

There are mechanisms for licensed businesses to serve at off-site events, including catering authorisations and daily licences, but these are generally obtained by an existing licensee such as a winery, brewery, or licensed caterer rather than by a host or an unlicensed bartending service.

If any service offers to sell you a package that includes the alcohol, ask which ABC licence they hold and under what authorisation they are serving at your venue. There may be a perfectly good answer. There may not.

Cash bars are the trap

The moment guests pay for drinks, even indirectly through drink tickets sold at the door, you are in the territory of an alcohol sale and you need proper licensing. Weddings and private parties almost always avoid this by hosting the bar. If you were planning a cash bar to offset costs, talk to your venue and confirm the licensing position before you build it into your budget.

Insurance is separate again

Certification is not insurance. A professional mobile bar should carry general liability coverage, and many venues will require a certificate of insurance naming them as additionally insured before they will let a vendor on site. Liquor liability coverage is a distinct product and worth asking about. None of this is legally mandated in the way RBS certification is, but a service without insurance is a service you should not hire, and your venue will probably make that decision for you anyway.

Local permits and venue rules

Beyond state requirements, individual cities and counties may impose permit conditions for events, particularly on public property, and venues have their own vendor rules. Sonoma and Marin venues frequently maintain approved vendor lists and require insurance certificates on file. None of that is a licensing question, but it will affect who you can hire, so check your venue contract early.

What to ask a bartending service

Four questions cover it. Are all bartenders working my event RBS-certified and currently registered with ABC? Do you carry general liability insurance, and can you provide a certificate for my venue? Do you hold a liquor licence, or is this a dry hire where I purchase the alcohol? Have you worked at my venue before, and are you on their approved vendor list?

A service that answers all four crisply is a service that has done this before.

A note on accuracy

Alcohol regulation changes, and enforcement details vary by jurisdiction. Everything above reflects how the rules generally work for private events in California, but for anything consequential, confirm directly with California ABC or your venue rather than relying on a vendor page, including this one.
