How far in advance should I book a bartender for a wedding?

Book your wedding bartender six to nine months before the date, and ten to twelve months if you are getting married on a Saturday between May and October in Sonoma County. Bar service is usually booked after the venue, photographer, and caterer, which is exactly why good bartenders are already gone by the time couples start looking.

The honest answer depends on your date

Not all Saturdays are equal. In Sonoma County, Marin, and San Francisco the peak stretches from May through October, with September and early October being the busiest weeks of the year because harvest season and reliable weather collide. A Saturday in that window is competitive, and the strongest vendors are frequently committed a year out.

A Friday or Sunday in the same season is meaningfully easier. A weekday, or anything between November and March, is easier still, and often cheaper across every vendor category, not just the bar.

A working timeline

  • Twelve months out: peak-season Saturday, large guest count, or a venue with a restricted approved vendor list. Book now.
  • Nine months out: the comfortable target for most Saturday weddings in the region.
  • Six months out: workable for most dates. You will still have real choice.
  • Three months out: possible, but your options narrow and you may be choosing on availability rather than fit.
  • Under six weeks: call anyway. Cancellations happen, and a service with a gap in the calendar would rather fill it.

Why bar service gets booked late

Couples work down a predictable list: venue, then photographer, then caterer, then flowers, then music. The bar tends to surface once the catering conversation reveals that alcohol is not included. By then it is often four months to the wedding.

There is a second reason, which is that many couples assume the caterer or venue handles the bar. Sometimes they do. Frequently they provide a surface and nothing else, or they provide staff who are not certified for alcohol service. It is worth asking that question the week you sign your catering contract rather than six months later.

What you need before you can book

Most services will hold a date with less information than you think. To get a firm quote and a contract you generally need your date, your venue, an approximate headcount, and a rough service window. You do not need your final guest list, your cocktail menu, or your timeline. Those get refined later, and a good service will build the menu with you closer to the date so it reflects what is actually in season.

Deposits are normal, typically a percentage of the total, and they are what actually holds the date. A verbal hold is not a booking.

Booking early buys you more than availability

The practical advantage of booking nine months out is not just that you get the team you want. It is that you get their input while decisions are still reversible. A bartender who sees your floor plan early can tell you that one bar for 160 guests will create a queue, that the spot you chose has no power, or that the load-in path involves a flight of stairs. Those conversations are cheap in February and expensive in August.

Early booking also locks pricing before seasonal rate changes, and it gives you time to build the alcohol shopping into your budget rather than absorbing it as a surprise.

If you are already late

Do not assume you are out of options. Ask about Friday and Sunday availability if your date is flexible. Be upfront about your headcount and budget so a service can tell you immediately whether it is workable rather than after three emails. Consider a simplified menu, because a beer, wine, and two-batched-cocktail bar is far easier to staff on short notice than a six-cocktail programme. And ask whether they can recommend someone if they genuinely cannot take it; reputable vendors in this region know each other.
