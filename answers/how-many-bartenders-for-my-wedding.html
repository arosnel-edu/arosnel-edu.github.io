How many bartenders do I need for my wedding?

Plan on one bartender for every 50 to 75 guests, which means most weddings of 100 to 150 people need two bartenders. Push toward the lower end of that ratio if you are serving signature cocktails, running a single bar, or expecting everyone to arrive at once.

Why the ratio matters more than the headcount

Guests do not drink at a steady rate. They arrive in waves, and the two worst moments are the minute cocktail hour opens and the minute dinner service ends. A bar staffed for the average will fail at the peak, and a twenty minute queue during cocktail hour is one of the few things guests genuinely remember and complain about.

The ratio exists to survive the peak, not the average. That is why it looks generous on paper.

The practical numbers

  • Up to 50 guests: one bartender is comfortable, assuming a simple menu.
  • 50 to 75 guests: one bartender works, two is noticeably smoother if you are pouring cocktails.
  • 75 to 150 guests: two bartenders.
  • 150 to 225 guests: three bartenders, ideally across two bar stations.
  • 225 and up: four or more, with multiple stations and usually a dedicated barback.

Our own packages follow this logic: the Spark package covers up to 50 guests with one bartender, and the Inferno package covers up to 150 with two.

What pushes you toward more staff

Several things make the same headcount harder to serve:

  • Craft cocktails. A shaken drink with fresh juice and a garnish takes three to four times as long as pouring a glass of wine. Two signature cocktails on the menu is the sweet spot; six is a queue.
  • A single bar location. One bar means every guest funnels through the same point. Two stations at opposite ends of the space cut the perceived wait dramatically, even with the same number of staff.
  • A hard start. If cocktail hour begins the moment a ceremony ends, all 150 people arrive within four minutes.
  • A tray-passed service style. Passed drinks require someone to make and someone to carry.
  • A spread-out venue. Vineyard and ranch venues in Sonoma often put the bar a long walk from prep space and ice.
  • No barback. If bartenders are also hauling ice, restocking, and cutting garnish, they are not pouring.

What lets you get away with fewer

A beer, wine, and seltzer bar with no cocktails moves at roughly double the speed of a craft cocktail bar. Pre-batched cocktails on tap or in dispensers are faster still. If you are doing a wine-country reception where most guests are drinking a local white and a local red, one bartender can genuinely handle a larger room than the ratio suggests.

Signature cocktails batched in advance are the best of both worlds: guests get something distinctive, and service stays quick because nothing is being built to order.

A realistic wedding timeline

For a 120 guest reception with two bartenders and two signature cocktails, a normal shape looks like this. Bar opens at cocktail hour and runs hard for sixty minutes, which is the busiest stretch of the night. Service slows sharply during dinner, when the team restocks and preps. It spikes again for roughly thirty minutes after dinner, then tapers through dancing until last call, typically thirty minutes before the end of the reception.

Five hours of bar service covers that comfortably. Four hours works if your ceremony and reception are tight, but it means the bar closes while people are still dancing.

Ask about certification, not just headcount

Two bartenders who are RBS-certified and experienced with event volume will outperform three who are not. In California, anyone serving alcohol needs Responsible Beverage Service certification, and event work in particular rewards people who can read a room and manage service without making it awkward. When you compare quotes, ask how many of the bartenders on your event will be certified and how many weddings they have worked, not just how many bodies are showing up.

The short version

Two bartenders for 100 to 150 guests, two bar stations if your floor plan allows it, two signature cocktails rather than six, and five hours rather than four. That combination handles almost every wedding we work in Sonoma County, Marin County, and San Francisco without a line forming.
