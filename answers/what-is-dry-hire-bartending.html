What is dry hire bartending?

Dry hire bartending means you buy the alcohol and the bartending service provides the trained, certified staff and the equipment to serve it. It is the standard model for private events in California, and it is usually the least expensive way to have a professional bar.

Where the term comes from

The word dry refers to the alcohol, not the service. In a dry hire arrangement the bartending company arrives with everything except the bottles, because it does not hold a liquor licence and therefore cannot legally sell alcohol. You own the product; they provide the skill, the tools, and the labour.

You will also hear this called a bartender-only booking, labour-only bar service, or BYOB bartending. They describe the same structure.

What is normally included

Scope varies, so this is the part to nail down in writing. A typical dry hire includes:

  • One or more RBS-certified bartenders for an agreed number of hours
  • Professional bar tools: shakers, strainers, jiggers, muddlers, bar mats, speed pourers
  • Setup and breakdown of the bar area
  • Responsible service, identification checks, and cut-off management
  • General liability insurance covering the staff working your event

At our entry tier, the Spark package covers up to 50 guests for four hours on exactly this basis, from $500.

What is usually not included

This is where surprises happen. Do not assume any of the following are covered unless the contract says so:

  • Alcohol. Always yours.
  • Mixers, juices, syrups, and garnishes. Often yours at the entry tier and included at higher tiers.
  • Ice. Frequently overlooked. Plan on roughly one and a half pounds per guest, more in summer.
  • The physical bar. A dry hire bartender may expect a surface to be waiting. Some venues provide one, some do not.
  • Glassware. Usually from the venue, the caterer, or a rental company.
  • Coolers and tubs. Ask directly.

Our Blaze and Inferno packages exist precisely because this list gets long. They fold the bar, coolers, ice, mixers, and garnishes into one number so nobody is making a hardware store run at four in the afternoon.

When dry hire is the right call

It is a genuinely good fit when your venue already has a built bar and glassware, when you have strong opinions about what gets poured, when your guest count is modest, or when you are working to a tight budget and are willing to do the shopping and hauling yourself. Wine country weddings where the couple wants specific local bottles on the list are a classic dry hire scenario.

It also has a real advantage on cost, because you pay retail for the product instead of a service markup, and you keep whatever is unopened.

When it is the wrong call

Dry hire stops being a bargain when you are counting the hours you spend on it. If you have more than about 75 guests, no venue bar, no easy way to move eight coolers of ice, or you want signature cocktails that require prepped syrups and fresh juice, the logistics quietly become a second job on the week you have the least time. That is the moment a full-service package is worth the difference.

It is also the wrong call if nobody in your party wants to be responsible for the shopping list, the ice run, and the leftover inventory at midnight.

The one thing not to compromise on

A cheaper hourly rate is not a saving if the person behind the bar is not certified and insured. In California, alcohol servers are required to hold Responsible Beverage Service certification, and a professional service will also carry general liability coverage. Ask for both. A bartender who will over-serve a guest because it feels awkward to say no is a genuine liability at a private event, and that judgment is the core of what you are hiring.

Questions worth asking before you book

Ask what exactly arrives with the bartender, who supplies ice and mixers, whether a bar surface is included, how many hours the rate covers and what an extra hour costs, whether travel to your venue is included, whether the staff are RBS-certified, and whether the company carries liability insurance. Six clear answers to those questions will tell you more about a service than any price comparison.
